Open source

LLM serving, measured fairly

Every engine comparison I found used a different model, GPU or memory setting. llm-serving-bench runs vLLM and SGLang (TensorRT-LLM supported) on the same GPU, model, prompts and memory budget, and times every streamed token from 1 to 64 concurrent users.

609 vs 384

tokens per second at 64 users, vLLM vs SGLang, same Tesla T4

4-bit AWQ made one user 2.3x faster but 64 users only 5% faster. On this GPU, quantization is a latency win, not a capacity win, which changes how you size a fleet.

02

Research ·
Quinnipiac, 2024 to 2026

GPU clusters and self-hosted LLMs for a research group

I ran Kubernetes GPU clusters with the NVIDIA GPU Operator and Cilium, and self-hosted Llama 3, Mistral, Gemma and DeepSeek with 4-bit quantization. CUDA out-of-memory failures kept taking models down, so I put them behind a process-isolated FastAPI server, which eliminated those failures.

For SPARK, our RAG framework, I benchmarked on-prem GPUs, GCP L4 and AWS Bedrock on the same workload. Average latency went from 30s to 15s to 10s, and the lab moved its inference to Bedrock.

The call I made
Recommend managed inference over our own GPUs once the benchmark showed it was faster for this workload, even though running those GPUs was my job.
What came of it
SPARK was published at IEEE UEMCON 2025, and the group grew from 2 to 10+ members across AI, quantum computing and cybersecurity.

Kernels, to understand where the time goes

llm-kernels has fused Triton kernels for softmax, RMSNorm and SwiGLU (forward and backward), and a tiled GEMM compared against cuBLAS. It is the inside view of what the benchmark shows: decode is memory-bound, which is also why quantization helped one user far more than 64.
